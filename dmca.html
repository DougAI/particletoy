Reporting infringement

Send a written notice to the agent above. Federal law sets out what it has to contain, and a notice missing these elements may not oblige us to act (17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3)(A)):

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work you say has been infringed — or, if the notice covers several works at one site, a representative list.
  3. Identification of the material you say is infringing, described well enough for us to find it. A link to the particle's page is the fastest way to do this.
  4. Your contact details — address, telephone number, and email address.
  5. A statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and — under penalty of perjury — that you are the copyright owner or authorized to act for them.

Section 512(f) makes it possible to be held liable for damages, including costs and legal fees, for knowingly misrepresenting that material is infringing. Fair use is a real thing; please consider whether it applies before sending a notice.

What happens next

On receiving a notice that complies with the requirements above, we expect to remove or disable access to the material, and to let whoever published it know what was removed and why — passing on a copy of the notice, including the contact details in it.

If your work was removed by mistake

If your material was taken down and you believe that was an error or a misidentification, you can send a counter-notification to the same agent. Again, the law sets out what it must contain (17 U.S.C. §512(g)(3)):

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed, and where it appeared before removal.
  3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, address and telephone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for your address — or, if your address is outside the United States, the United States District Court for the District of Maryland, where Particle Toy LLC may be found — and that you'll accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice, or their agent.

We expect to forward a valid counter-notification to whoever sent the original notice, and to tell them the material may be restored in 10 business days — unless they first tell us they've filed a court action to stop it. Restoration would then normally follow between 10 and 14 business days after we receive the counter-notification.

A counter-notification hands your name and address to the person who filed the notice against you, and puts you in front of a federal court if they choose to sue. It's worth talking to a lawyer before sending one.

Repeat infringers

It is our policy to terminate, in appropriate circumstances, the accounts of users who repeatedly infringe the copyright of others. We take account of valid counter-notifications, and of notices that turn out to be mistaken or misdirected, in deciding what is appropriate.
